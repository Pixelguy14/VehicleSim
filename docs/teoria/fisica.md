The Core Physics EquationsRigid Body MotionFor a body with mass $m$, moment of inertia $I$, position $\vec{p}$, velocity $\vec{v}$, orientation angle $\theta$, and angular velocity $\omega$:   Summing Forces & Torques:$$\vec{F}_{\text{total}} = \sum \vec{F}_i, \quad \tau_{\text{total}} = \sum (\vec{r}_i \times \vec{F}_i) = \sum (r_{ix} F_{iy} - r_{iy} F_{ix})$$(where $\vec{r}_i$ is the offset vector from the body's center of mass to the force application point).   Linear & Angular Acceleration:$$\vec{a} = \frac{\vec{F}_{\text{total}}}{m}, \quad \alpha = \frac{\tau_{\text{total}}}{I}$$Semi-Implicit Euler Integration (over fixed timestep $\Delta t$):$$\vec{v}_{t+\Delta t} = \vec{v}_t + \vec{a} \cdot \Delta t$$$$\vec{p}_{t+\Delta t} = \vec{p}_t + \vec{v}_{t+\Delta t} \cdot \Delta t$$$$\omega_{t+\Delta t} = \omega_t + \alpha \cdot \Delta t$$$$\theta_{t+\Delta t} = \theta_t + \omega_{t+\Delta t} \cdot \Delta t$$Local Point VelocityTo calculate forces or contact responses at any specific point on the body (e.g., a wheel or a bumper corner at local position $\vec{r}$):   $$\vec{v}_{\text{point}} = \vec{v}_{\text{body}} + \vec{\omega} \times \vec{r} = \begin{bmatrix} v_x - \omega \cdot r_y \\ v_y + \omega \cdot r_x \end{bmatrix}$$The 2D Tyre Model (Car Dynamics)The defining feel of top-down drifting comes from how tyre forces are split. For each wheel:Get Wheel Heading Vector:$$\hat{u}_{\text{forward}} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos(\theta + \delta) \\ \sin(\theta + \delta) \end{bmatrix}, \quad \hat{u}_{\text{side}} = \begin{bmatrix} -\sin(\theta + \delta) \\ \cos(\theta + \delta) \end{bmatrix}$$(where $\delta$ is the front steering angle).Decompose Local Velocity:$$v_{\text{forward}} = \vec{v}_{\text{wheel}} \cdot \hat{u}_{\text{forward}}$$$$v_{\text{side}} = \vec{v}_{\text{wheel}} \cdot \hat{u}_{\text{side}}$$Apply Asymmetric Friction Forces:$$\vec{F}_{\text{tyre}} = - (k_{\text{rolling}} \cdot v_{\text{forward}}) \hat{u}_{\text{forward}} - (k_{\text{lateral}} \cdot v_{\text{side}}) \hat{u}_{\text{side}}$$$k_{\text{lateral}} \gg k_{\text{rolling}}$: High side resistance keeps the car glued when turning.   Handbrake/Drifting: Holding Spacebar drops $k_{\text{lateral}}$ on rear wheels drastically and boosts $k_{\text{rolling}}$, breaking rear traction and causing controllable oversteer spin.   

To simulate realistic collisions against static walls—where a vehicle bounces, slides, and rotates upon impact—we use the sequential contact impulse formulation.Instead of moving the vehicle out of the wall and reversing its overall velocity, we calculate a instantaneous restitution impulse ($j_N$) perpendicular to the wall and a friction impulse ($j_T$) parallel to the wall, applied directly at the contact point.1. Mathematical FormulationRelative Contact Point VelocityWhen a vehicle corner at relative offset $\vec{r}$ hits a wall with normal vector $\hat{N}$, the velocity of that specific corner is:$$\vec{v}_{\text{contact}} = \vec{v} + \vec{\omega} \times \vec{r} = \begin{bmatrix} v_x - \omega r_y \\ v_y + \omega r_x \end{bmatrix}$$The normal relative velocity (speed moving into the wall) is:$$v_N = \vec{v}_{\text{contact}} \cdot \hat{N}$$If $v_N \ge 0$, the point is moving away from or parallel to the wall—no collision impulse is required.Normal Contact Impulse ($j_N$)To stop penetration and cause a rebound proportional to the coefficient of restitution ($e \in [0, 1]$):$$j_N = \frac{-(1 + e) \cdot v_N}{\frac{1}{m} + \frac{(\vec{r} \times \hat{N})^2}{I}}$$where:$m$ is mass, $I$ is moment of inertia.$\vec{r} \times \hat{N} = r_x N_y - r_y N_x$ (2D cross product scalar).Tangential Friction Impulse ($j_T$)To simulate wall grinding and surface friction during impact, calculate velocity along the wall's tangent vector $\hat{T}$ (where $\hat{T} = [-N_y, N_x]^T$):$$v_T = \vec{v}_{\text{contact}} \cdot \hat{T}$$$$j_T^{\text{ideal}} = \frac{-v_T}{\frac{1}{m} + \frac{(\vec{r} \times \hat{T})^2}{I}}$$According to Coulomb's Law of Friction, the friction impulse cannot exceed the normal force scaled by coefficient of friction $\mu$:$$\vert{}j_T\vert{} = \min(\vert{}j_T^{\text{ideal}}\vert{}, \mu \cdot j_N)$$Applying Impulses to the StateOnce $j_N$ and $j_T$ are computed, the net impulse vector applied at point $\vec{r}$ is:$$\vec{J} = j_N \hat{N} + j_T \hat{T}$$Update linear and angular velocities instantaneously:$$\vec{v}_{\text{new}} = \vec{v}_{\text{old}} + \frac{\vec{J}}{m}$$$$\omega_{\text{new}} = \omega_{\text{old}} + \frac{\vec{r} \times \vec{J}}{I}$$
